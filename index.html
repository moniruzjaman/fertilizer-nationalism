Part I · The Opportunity

Two pictures. One distance. That distance is the reform.

On the national ledger, abundance is the story: 1.365 million tonnes in warehouses and 365,000 tonnes of approved imports worth Tk 2,562 crore moving smoothly. At the farm gate, the next chapter is being written — in premiums, queues and uncertainty. Hold the two pictures side by side and the value of reform becomes unmistakable.

Precision is justice

The same budget. Exponentially better outcomes.

World Bank assessment: the top quintile of landholders currently receives roughly half of every taka spent, while the bottom 40 percent of farmers — the families who turn every taka into food security — receive around one seventh. Redirecting the flow multiplies yield, income and national food supply.

Fiscal excellence

At Tk 17,001 Cr/yr (+ Tk 1,350 Cr in FY2025-26), subsidies are ~80% of public ag spending. Every efficiency gain releases fresh resources for extension, research and rural infrastructure.

Precision targeting

Environmental stewardship

Part II · The Turning Point

Asia has already paid for these lessons. Bangladesh gets them free.

Efficiency is the new yield. China's nutrient use efficiency slid 61%→50%, India's 50%→42% — while India spends over US$8 billion a year. Bangladesh can lead the region by holding NUE above 50%, a distinction no large Asian economy has achieved at scale.

The bloom, lived

A morning in Bhurungamari, after the reform.

Policy is only worth what a farmer can hold. This is the end-state, step by step.

She walks to the authorized dealer

One dealer per union under the 2026 Comprehensive Dealer Policy — streamlined, transparent, digitally monitored.

She places her thumb on the scanner

Krishak Card + NID biometric authentication at the point of sale, with offline capability and local server sync.

Her exact entitlement appears

Urea and DAP quantities calculated from her soil-test results and crop declaration, linked to her verified land parcel.

She pays the government-fixed price

No queue. No black market. No uncertainty. The transaction takes under ten minutes.

The ledger closes the loop

Every kilogram traced from import or production to final sale on a national digital ledger — with a public dashboard behind it.

Part V · Implementation

Sixty months. Four phases. Each capability proven before the next depends on it.

Phase I · Foundation · Months 1–12

Regulatory & technical base

Implementing rules of the 2026 Dealer Policy with digital compliance mandates; biometric PoS pilots in 2 divisions; Krishak Card ↔ NID ↔ initial BDS parcels; Soil Health Card sampling in pilot upazilas; local server nodes; KrishiChain Phase 1 (50 dealers, 95% capture).

Phase II · Integration · Months 13–24

Nationwide authentication

Nationwide biometric PoS and e-Bill on the India model; quotas linked to verified land area and crop type; mobile crop-declaration and pre-booking app; satellite NDVI verification for Boro; public transparency dashboard; KrishiChain Phase 2.

Phase III · Optimization · Months 25–36

Subsidy moves to the farmer

e-Voucher pilots over MFS rails; Soil Health Card coverage beyond one million; AI/ML demand forecasting; import share falls from 70% toward 50%; KrishiChain Phase 3 (5,000 dealers, +10% NUE); BADC MIS fully integrated.

Phase IV · Sustainability · Months 37–60

The self-sustaining cycle

Full transition to targeted, land-linked entitlements; market-linkage platforms rewarding sustainable practice; independent oversight body; full SDG 2.4 and FAO Better Production alignment; South Asian agricultural-governance leadership secured.

The success backbone

Every outcome carries a number. Every number carries a name.

Quarterly Ministry-led technical reviews with DAE, BADC and IT teams; an annual stakeholder forum with farmers and civil society; smart support triggers — e.g. local-server redundancy whenever PoS uptime approaches 90%.

MEL results framework — switch the horizon

Fiscal integrity: subsidy leakage moves High → <10% (Y3) → <5% (Y5) via audit + blockchain trace. Sources: Central Digital Ledger, Krishak Card + MFS data, iFMS-style system, soil + yield data, farmer surveys.

Part VI · Cost–benefit

The reform pays for itself. Then it pays again. Every year.

Efficiency recovery

Tk 2,000–3,000 crore per year, against a capital requirement limited to biometric PoS devices, local servers and soil-testing expansion — all scalable through public-private partnership.

Payback & permanent dividend

Repayment within two to three fiscal cycles, after which savings compound every year the system runs. Farmer transaction time falls from a morning at the counter to under 10 minutes.
